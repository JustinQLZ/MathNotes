#Algebraic-Geometry 
**Definition.** Let $f:X\dashrightarrow Y$ be a rational morphism of schemes of finite type over a field $k$. Then $\mathrm{Dom}(f)$ (the domain of $f$) is defined to be the maximal open subset of $X$ on which $f$ is defined. So $x\in\mathrm{Dom}(f)$ if and only if on an open neighborhood of $x\in X$ $f$ is defined.

This definition is reasonable. It suffices to consider rational functions $f\in K(X)$. Locally on $X$, say $\mathrm{Spec}\,A\subset X$, $K(X)=\mathrm{Frac}\,A$. So $f$ is expressed as a quotient of elements in $A$. The major open subset defined by the denominator of $f$ over $\mathrm{Spec}\,A$ is an open subset on which $f$ is defined. On the intersection of two open subsets, $f$ is of course compatible because it is an element in $K(X)$. So $\mathrm{Dom}(f)$ is the union of all such open subsets.

**Proposition.** Let $f:X\dashrightarrow Y$ be a rational morphism of schemes of finite type over a field $k$. Suppose $X$ is irreducible, with generic point $\xi$ and $\eta=f(\xi)$. Let $x\in X$. Then $x\in\mathrm{Dom}(f)$ if and only if $\exists y\in Y$ and a ring homomorphism $\mathcal{O}_{y,Y}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{x, X}$ extending $f^\#:\mathcal{O}_{Y,\eta}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X,\xi}$, i.e.

is commutative.
**Proof.** The only if part is trivial. The if part follows from following arguments over rings. Let $A$ be a finitely generated $k$-algebra, say generated by $a_1,\dots,a_n$. Let $B_\mathfrak{p}=\mathcal{O}_{Y,y}$. Given a $k$-homomorphism of rings $A\rightarrow B_\mathfrak{p}$, let $a_i$ be mapped to $b_i/s_i$. Then this homomorphism lifts to $A\rightarrow B[1/s_1\cdots s_n]$.$$\tag*{$\square$}$$
