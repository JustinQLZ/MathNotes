#Algebraic-Geometry 
Here I collect several ideas used to extend morphisms.

#### Smooth Variety to Abelian Variety
In this section, a variety means a separated, geometrically integral scheme of finite type over a fixed field $k$.
**Theorem.** Let $X$ be an abelian variety over a field $k$. If $V$ is a smooth $k$-variety, then any rational map $f:V\dashrightarrow X$ extends to a morphism $V\rightarrow X$.
**Proof.** We may assume that $k=\bar{k}$, for if a morphism $V_\bar{k}\rightarrow X_\bar{k}$ is defined over $k$ on some dense open subset of $V_\bar{k}$, then it is defined over $k$. (Descent iff $\forall\sigma\in\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{k}/k))\,\sigma(f)=f$. Recall that if two morphisms from a separated scheme are identical on a dense open subset, then they are identical on the whole scheme. This is also used in the last step of the proof of the rigidity lemma. See [[Liouville's Theorem and Rigidity Lemma]].) Let $\mathrm{Dom}(f)=U$. (See [[Domain of a Rational Map]].) We need to show that $U=V$.
###### Step 1. Extension by Properness.
For any codimension 1 point $P\in X$, the local ring $\mathcal{O}_{V,P}$ is a DVR since $V$ is smooth over $k$. Since $X$ is an abelian variety, it is proper over $k$. So by the valuative criterion of properness, $\mathrm{Spec}\,K(V)$ uniquely extends to a morphism $\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathcal{O}_{V,P}\rightarrow X$. Then by the arguments on rings below, because $X$ is locally of finite type over $k$, this last morphism further extends to a morphism $Y\rightarrow X$ for some open $Y\subset V$ containing $P$. Hence $\mathrm{codim}(X-\mathrm{Dom}(f),X)\geq 2$.
The arguments on the rings are as followings. Let $A$ be a finitely generated $k$-algebra, say generated by $a_1,\dots,a_n$. Let $B_\mathfrak{p}=\mathcal{O}_{V,P}$. Given a $k$-homomorphism of rings $A\rightarrow B_\mathfrak{p}$, let $a_i$ be mapped to $b_i/s_i$. Then this homomorphism lifts to $A\rightarrow B[1/s_1\cdots s_n]$.
###### Step 2. Conversion to $F(u,v)=f(u)-f(v)$.
**Claim.** $v\in\mathrm{Dom}(f)$ if and only if $(v,v)\in\mathrm{Dom}(F)$.
Let $W=\mathrm{Dom}(F)$. The only if part is trivial. Now suppose $(v,v)\in\mathrm{Dom}(F)$, then $(V\times\{v\})\cap W$ is an open subset of $V=V\times\{v\}$ containing $v$. Hence we can choose $u\in U(k)$ such that $(u,v)\in W$. Then ($\{u\}\times V)\cap W$ is an open subset of $V=\{u\}\times V$ containing $v$, on which $f$ is defined because we have $f(w)=f(u)-F(u,w)$.
###### Step 3. Identification of the Degeneration Locus.
The benefit of step 2 is that $F$ maps the diagonal to a fixed point $0\in X$ and hence $(v,v)\in\mathrm{Dom}(F)$ if and only if $F^\#$ maps $\mathcal{O}_{X,0}\subset K(X)$ into $\mathcal{O}_{V\times V,(v,v)}$. Suppose that $v\notin\mathrm{Dom}(f)$, where $v\in V(k)$, and choose an element $\varphi\in\mathcal{O}_{X,0}$ such that $F^\#(\varphi)\notin\mathcal{O}_{V\times V,(v,v)}$. Let $D$ be the polar divisor of $F^\#(\varphi)$, i.e.$$
D=\sum_{\substack{A\subset V\times V\text{ a variety}\\ \mathrm{codim}(A,V\times V)=1\\ \mathrm{ord}_A(F^\#(\varphi))<0}}\mathrm{ord_A}(F^\#(\varphi))[A].
$$So for any $w\in V(k)$, $w\notin\mathrm{Dom}(f)$ if and only if $(w,w)\in\Delta\cap|D|$. Since $V$ is smooth over $k$, $D$ is locally principal, and hence $\Delta\cap|D|$ is also locally principal. So $\Delta\cap|D|$ has codimension $\leq 1$ in $\Delta$. Hence $f$ is not regular on a subset of $V$ of codimension $\leq 1$, contradicting our earlier conclusion in step 1.$$\tag*{$\square$}$$**Remarks.**
1. Intuitively, let $v_0\in\partial\mathrm{Dom}(f)$ with open neighborhood $U$ and $w\in U\cap\mathrm{Dom}(f)$, then for any $v\in U$, $f(v)-f(w)$ is in an open neighborhood of $0\in X$.
2. Notice in step 1 that the extension from $\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathcal{O}_{V,v}\rightarrow X$ to an open neighborhood of $v$ only requires $X$ being locally of finite type over $k$. This motivates the proposition in [[Domain of a Rational Map]].
3. The conversion from $f$ to $F$ is at the core of this proof. The key here is that $F$ factors through an affine scheme $\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathcal{O}_{X,0}$, so we can use the proposition in [[Domain of a Rational Map]] with $Y=\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathcal{O}_{X,0}$. If we try to use this proposition to $f$ itself, then we can't find a single $\varphi$ whose polar divisor lies in the degeneration part. In general, suppose we can convert from $f$ to $F:Z\rightarrow Y$ where $Y=\mathrm{Spec}\,A$ is an affine $k$-scheme. Then the polar divisor of the pullback of any regular function in $A$ along $F$ is contained in the degeneration part of $F$. And then we can convert back to $f$. (The key difficulty is that $X$ doesn't have any nontrivial regular function. So for an affine $Y$, the same trick applies.)
4. After conversion to $F$, we need to consider $\Delta\cap |D|$. It is a interesting phenomenon that $D$ being locally principal helps to guarantee that $\Delta\cap|D|$ has codimension $\geq1$ in $\Delta$.
