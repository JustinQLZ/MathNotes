This relies on a lemma:
**Lemma.** Let $X$ be an affine variety, then its principal open sets (i.e. $D(f)$ for $f\in A(X)$) are affine.
**Proof.** The proof is another application of Rabinowitsch trick, see [[Proofs of Hilbert's Nullensatz]]. Suppose $X\subset\mathbb{A}^n$. Consider the ideal $J=<I(X),1-y\tilde{f}>\subset k[x_1,\dots,x_n,y]$ where $\tilde{f}$ is any polynomial in $k[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ which represents $f$. Now let $Z=Z(J)$, we claim that $Z$ is isomorphic to $D(f)$. Consider the projection $\pi:\mathbb{A}^{n+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^n$ and let $\phi={\pi\bigr|}_Z$. Verify that the image of $\phi$ is contained in $X$. Also define $\psi:D(f)\rightarrow Z$ as $\psi(a_1,\dots,a_n)=(a_1,\dots,a_n,1/f(a_1,\dots,a_n))$. Verify that $\psi$ is well-defined and $\psi$ and $\phi$ are inverses. Since these're defined by polynomials, they're clearly morphisms. Now since $D(f)$ is an open subset of an irreducible space, it is also irreducible and hence $Z$ is an affine variety. $$\tag*{$\square$}$$By the way, there's a corollary (Hartshorne, Ex. I.3.5)
**Corollary.** Let $H\subset\mathbb{P}^n$ be a hypersurface, then $\mathbb{P}^n-H$ is affine.
**Proof.** Suppose $H$ is of degree $d$, use $d$-uple embedding (see Hartshorne, Ex. I.2.12, also [[About a Property of the Veronese Surface]]) to show that $\mathbb{P}^n-H$ can be considered as a subvariety of $\mathbb{P}^N-H'$, where $H'$ is a hyperplane. Notice that $\mathbb{P}^N-H'$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{A}^N$ then apply the lemma.$$\tag*{$\square$}$$**Lemma.** Let $X$ be any variety and let $P\in X$, then there exists an affine open nbhd of $P$.
**Proof.** When $X$ is quasi-affine, this is trivial. When $X$ is quasi-projective, by definition, $X\subset\bar{X}\subset\mathbb{P}^n$ for some $n$. Let $C=\bar{X}-X$, then $C$ is closed in $\bar{X}$ and hence is closed in $\mathbb{P}^n$. Since $P\notin C$, there exists a homogeneous polynomial $F$ which vanishes on $C$ but $F(P)\neq0$. Take $P=[p_0,\dots,p_n]$, and, WLOG, assume that $p_0\neq0$, then $P\in U_0$. Take $Y=U_0\cap \bar{X}$, then $Y$ is affine, open in $X$ and contains $P$. Let $f=F(1,x1/x_0,\dots,x_n/x_0)$ and consider $\bar{f}\in A(Y)$. Then $\bar{f}(P)\neq0$. Take $U=D(\bar{f})$ then $U$ is an affine open nbhd of $P$ in $Y$. Since $Y$ is open in $\bar{X}$, $U$ is also open in $\bar{X}$. By the choice of $F$, clearly $U\cap C=\varnothing$. Hence $U\subset X$ and is an affine open hbhd of $P$ in $X$.$$\tag*{$\square$}$$This is the exercise I.3.11 from Hartshorne.
**Theorem.** Let $X$ be any variety and let $P\in X$. Then there is a 1-1 correspondence between $\text{Spec}\,\mathcal{O}_{P,X}$ and closed subvarieties of $X$ containing $P$.
**Proof.** First show that the problem is essentially local. By the definition of $\mathcal{O}_{P,X}$, for any open subset $U\subset X$ containing $P$, we have $\mathcal{O}_{P,X}\cong\mathcal{O}_{P,U}$. Also, there's a 1-1 correspondence between closed subvarieties of $X$ and closed subvarieties of $U$ given by $V\mapsto V\cap U$, by which the property of containing $P$ is preserved. (verified) By taking $U$ as an ==affine open nbhd== of $P$, we see that it suffices to prove for the case when $X$ is affine. But this is a well-known result.$$\tag*{$\square$}$$The second part of Hartshorne, Ex. I.3.13 is another good example. Dimension is another local property. Let $X$ be a variety, $U$ its open subset, $P\in X$, we have $\text{dim}\,\mathcal{O}_{P,X}=\text{dim}\,U-\text{dim}\,X$.
**Theorem.** Let $X$ be a variety and $Y$ its subvariety, then $\text{dim}\,\mathcal{O}_{Y,X}=\text{dim}\,X-\text{dim}\,Y$.
**Proof.** Let $U$ be an open subset of $X$ such that $Y'=U\cap Y\neq\varnothing$, we'll show that $\mathcal{O}_{Y,X}=\mathcal{O}_{Y',U}$. Since $U$ is dense in $X$, every element of $\mathcal{O}_{Y,X}$ can be represented by an element of with domain contained in $U$, so $\mathcal{O}_{Y',U}\subset\mathcal{O}_{Y,X}$. Also, since $Y$ is irreducible, for any open subset $V$ of $U$ such that $V\cap Y\neq\varnothing$, we have $V\cap Y'\neq\varnothing$, hence $\mathcal{O}_{Y,X}=\mathcal{O}_{Y',U}$. Now since $\text{dim}\,X=\text{dim}\,U$ and $\text{dim}\,Y=\text{dim}\,Y'$, it suffices to prove for $Y'$ and $U$. Taking a point $P\in Y$ and let $U$ be an affine open nbhd of $P$ in $X$, we can assume that $X$ is affine in the first place. Also, since $U\cap\bar{Y}\neq\varnothing\implies U\cap Y\neq\varnothing$, we have $\mathcal{O}_{Y,X}=\mathcal{O}_{\bar{Y},U}$. Besides, we have $\text{dim}\,Y=\text{dim}\,\bar{Y}$. So, WLOG, we may assume that $Y$ is also affine.
Since $K(X)=\text{Frac}\,A(X)$, $\mathcal{O}_{Y,X}$ can be identified with the subring of $\text{Frac}\,A(X)$ whose elements are of the form $f/g$ with $D(g)\cap Y\neq\varnothing$, i.e. $g\notin I_X(Y)$. Hence $\mathcal{O}_{Y,X}=A(X)_{I_X{Y}}$ and $\text{dim}\,\mathcal{O}_{X,Y}=\text{ht}\,I_X(Y)=\text{dim}\,A(X)-\text{dim}\,A(Y)=\text{dim}\,X-\text{dim}\,Y$.$$\tag*{$\square$}$$
Actually, when the problem concerns finitely many points, we may still be able to reduce to the affine case.
**Lemma.** Let $Y$ be a quasi-projective variety, let $P_1,\dots,P_m\in Y$, then there exists an affine open subset $U$ of $Y$ containing both points.
**Proof.** Embed $Y$ in one of its projective closure $\bar{Y}=\text{Proj}\,S$ and let $Z=\bar{Y}-Y=Z(\mathfrak{a})$. Suppose the points correspond to maximal ideals $\mathfrak{m}_1,\dots,\mathfrak{m_m}\subset k[x_0,\dots,x_n]$, then it becomes a pure algebraic problem: find an element $f\in\mathfrak{a}-\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{m}\mathfrak{m_i}$, which does exist by the prime avoidance. Then take $U=D_+(f)$, which is affine since $\cong\text{Spec}\,S_{(f)}$ and contained in $Y$ since $D_+(f)\cap Z=\varnothing$.$$\tag*{$\square$}$$**Remark.** This property is called "finite affiness".
