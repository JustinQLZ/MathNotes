The proof follows steps split by Hartshorne III. Ex. 4.2. First we state the main theorem:
**Theorem.** (Chevalley) Let $f:X\rightarrow Y$ be a finite surjective morphism of noetherian separated schemes, with $X$ affine. Then $Y$ is affine.

**Lemma 1.** Let $X$ be a noetherian scheme, then $X$ is affine if and only if $X_{red}$ is affine.
**Proof.** One way is clear. Conversely, suppose $X_{red}$ is affine. Let $\mathcal{N}$ be the sheaf of nilpotent elements on $X$. For any coherent sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ on $X$, consider the filtration$$\mathcal{F}\supset\mathcal{N}\cdot\mathcal{F}\supset\mathcal{N}^2\cdot\mathcal{F}\supset\cdots.$$Since $\mathcal{F}$ is coherent and $X$ is noetherian, there exists some $n$ such that $\mathcal{N}^n\cdot\mathcal{F}=0$. Let $\mathcal{Q_k}=\mathcal{N}^k\cdot\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{N}^{k+1}\cdot\mathcal{F}$, then $\mathcal{Q}_k$ are coherent sheaves on $X_{red}$ and hence $H^i(X,\mathcal{Q}_k)=0$ for $i>0$. Then by induction on $k$ we see that $H^i(X,\mathcal{F})=0$ for $i>0$ and hence by Serre's criterion $X$ is affine.$$\tag*{$\square$}$$**Remark.** Get used to the powerful ideal of bringing constructions for the affine case to the general case!
**Lemma 2.** Let $X$ be a reduced noetherian scheme, then $X$ is affine if and only every irreducible component of $X$ is affine.
**Proof.** See [[One View of Fiber Product]].$$\tag*{$\square$}$$**Lemma 3.** Let $f:X\rightarrow Y$ be a finite surjective morphism of integral noetherian schemes.
1. $f^{-1}(\eta_Y)=\eta_X$ and $K(X)$ is a finite field extension over $K(Y)$.
2. Let $Z$ be any integral closed subscheme of $Y$, then there exists a integral closed subscheme $Z'$ of $X$ such that $f(Z')=Z$ and $f|_{Z'}:Z'\rightarrow Z$ is finite.
**Proof.** These're direct results of the affine case, which follows from incomparability of fibers of a finite morphism and the fact that $f$ is surjective.$$\tag*{$\square$}$$With these lemmas, we first reduce the proof to a special case:
**Case $\Delta$.** Further assume that $X$ and $Y$ are integral schemes and all proper closed subschemes of $Y$ are affine.
**Proof.** By Lemma 1 it's easy to reduced to the case $X$ and $Y$ are reduced. By Lemma 2, we can assume that $Y$ is integral. Then by 2 of  Lemma 3, we can further assume that $X$ is also integral. Then by noetherian induction the proof is reduced to the special case.$$\tag*{$\square$}$$**Remark.** Noetherian induction is a very useful tool for affiness, since closed subschemes of an affine scheme are all affine.
**Lemma 4.** Let $f:X\rightarrow Y$ be a finite surjective morphism of integral noetherian schemes, then there is a coherent sheaf $\mathcal{M}$ on $X$, and a morphism of sheaves $\alpha:\mathcal{O}_Y^r\rightarrow f_*\mathcal{M}$ for some $r>0$, such that $\alpha$ is an isomorphism at the generic point of $Y$.
**Proof.** Let $\mathcal{K}_X$ be the constant sheaf of $K(X)$ on $X$, which is quasi-coherent. Let $r=[K(X):K(Y)]<\infty$ (1 of Lemma 3) and choose a basis $u_1,\dots,u_r$. Then let $\mathcal{M}=\sum_i\mathcal{O}_Xu_i\subset\mathcal{K}_X$. The stalk of $f_*\mathcal{M}$ at the generic point of $Y$ is just $K(X)$.$$\tag*{$\square$}$$**Lemma 5.** Let $X, Y$ and $f$ be as in Lemma 4, then for any coherent sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ on $Y$, there exists a coherent sheaf $\mathcal{G}$ on $X$ and a morphism $\beta:f_*\mathcal{G}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}^r$, which is an isomorphism at the generic point of $Y$.
**Proof.** Apply $\mathcal{Hom}(\cdot,\mathcal{F})$ to $\alpha$ in Lemma 4 and use Hartshorne II. Ex. 5.17.e. Notice that taking stalk commutes with $\mathcal{Hom}$ here, see[[Basic Category Theory for Algebraic Geometry]]$$\tag*{$\square$}$$**Lemma 6.** Let $f:X\rightarrow Y$ be an affine morphism of noetherian separated schemes, then for any quasi-coherent sheaf $\mathcal{F}$ on $X$, there're natural isomorphisms for all $i\geq0$, $$H^i(X,\mathcal{F})\cong H^i(Y,f_*\mathcal{F}).$$**Proof.** Choose an affine open cover of $Y$. Pull it back to give an affine open cover of $X$. Then calculate cohomology via Čech cohomology.$$\tag*{$\square$}$$Now we can finish the proof:
**Proof of Chevalley's Theorem.** Assume case $\Delta$ above. Given any coherent sheaf on $Y$ we construct $\beta$ as in Lemma 5. Notice that since $\beta$ is an isomorphism at the generic point of $Y$, supports of $\mathrm{coker}\,\beta$ and $\mathrm{ker}\,\beta$ are proper subsets of $Y$. These're coherent sheaves on a noetherian scheme, so the supports have natural closed subscheme structure. Then since these supports are affine, the cohomology groups of them vanish for $i>0$. Finally by considering corresponding long exact sequences of$$
\begin{align}
&0\longrightarrow \mathrm{ker}\,\beta\longrightarrow f_*\mathcal{G}\longrightarrow\mathrm{im}\,\beta\longrightarrow0\\
&0\longrightarrow \mathrm{im}\,\beta\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}\longrightarrow\mathrm{coker}\,\beta\longrightarrow0
\end{align}
$$we see that $H^1(Y,\mathcal{F})=0$. Therefore by Serre's criterion, $Y$ is affine.$$\tag*{$\square$}$$**Remark.** A coherent sheaf on a noetherian scheme have a closed support and this support has a natural closed subscheme structure!

This proof is my solution for the Hartshorne exercise. The solution is all done independently following the instructions of Hartshorne except for Lemma 4.
For lemma 4 the key to notice that $\alpha$ is essentially a choice of $r$ global sections of $\mathcal{M}$, another generalization of a familiar idea in the affine case to general schemes. Also I proved Lemma 3 independently, and struggled on lack of tools extending a rational morphism of sheaves to the whole scheme. This again teaches me the lesson that ==when in struggle, try to solve the struggle in the first place==, i.e. rather than extending, try to give a morphism in priori.
