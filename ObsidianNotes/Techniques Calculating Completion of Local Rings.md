Hartshorne, Ex. I.5.1 provides four curves $\mathbb{A}^2$ to calculate. (Assume $\text{char}\,k\neq2$.)
	(a). $x^2=x^4+y^4$    (tacnode)
	(b). $xy=x^6+y^6$    (node)
	(c). $x^3=y^2+x^4+y^4$    (cusp)
	(d). $x^2y+xy^2=x^4+y^4$    (triple point)
Our goal is to calculate the completions of local rings at the singular points on these curves (which are all $(0,0)$) and to see how their ring structures are related to their local geometric properties.
First we need a lemma:
**Lemma.** Let $f\in k[x_1,\dots,x_n]$, then the completion (with respect to the maximal ideal) of $k[x_1,\dots,x_n]_{(x,y)}/(f)$ is $k[[x_1,\dots,x_n]]/(f)$.
**Proof.** This is because completion is exact if the ring is noetherian and the completion of $(f)$ is clearly $f\,k[[x_1,\dots,x_n]]$.$$\tag*{$\square$}$$Generalizing the idea of Hartshorne (I.5.6.3), we have:
**Proposition.** Let $f=f_r+f_{r+1}+\cdots\in k[[x,y]]$ (only two indeterminants!) such that $f_r=g_sh_t$ where $g_s$ and $h_t$ are homogeneous polynomials with no common linear factors, then their are power series $g=g_s+g_{s+1}+\cdots$ and $h=h_t+h_{t+1}+\cdots$ such that $f=gh$.
**Proof.** By induction, this only use the key fact that$$g_sA+h_tB=E_{r+l}$$is solvable for a homogeneous polynomial $E_{r+l}$ of degree $r+l$. Let $R=k[x,y]$ and $I=(g_s,h_t)$. Consider the $R$-module epimorphism map $\phi:R\oplus R\rightarrow I$ given by $\phi(u,v)=ug_s+vh_t$, then since $R$ is a UFD, its kernel is just the free $R$-module generated by $(h_t,g_s)$. So we have an s.e.s.$$0\rightarrow R\rightarrow R\oplus R\rightarrow I\rightarrow 0$$In dimension $r+k$, we have$$0\rightarrow R_{l}\rightarrow R_{s+l}\oplus R_{t+l}\rightarrow I_{r+l}\rightarrow 0$$So$$
\text{dim}_k\,I_{r+k}=\text{dim}_k\,R_{s+l}\oplus R_{t+l}-\text{dim}_k\,R_l
$$and when $n=2$, we find that $\text{dim}_k\,I_{r+l}=r+l+1=\text{dim}_k\,R_{r+l}$, so $I_{r+l}=R_{r+l}$. Notice that this fails for $n>2$.$$\tag*{$\square$}$$Here's a simple but useful tool that could help us to factor $f$:
**Proposition.** If the form of lowest degree of $f\in k[[x_1,\dots,x_n]]$ is irreducible in $k[x_1,\dots,x_n]$, then $f$ is irreducible. (The proof is trivial.)
Also here're powerful techniques developed by Weierstrass (first for holomorphic function germs, see detailed proofs at [[Weierstrass Preparation.pdf]]):
**Definition 1.** A polynomial $P\in k[[x]][y]$ (denoting $x=x_1,\dots,x_n$ formally) is called a Weierstrass polynomial of degree $d$ in $y$ if it has the form$$P(x,y)=y^d+a_1(x)y^{d-1}+\cdots+a_d(x)$$where it is monic in $y$ and each coefficient lies in $(x_1,\dots,x_n)\subset k[[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ (i.e. $a_i(0)=0$).
**Theorem 1. (Weierstrass Division Theorem)** Let $k$ be a field and let $f\in k[[x,y]]$ be of the form $f=uy^s-w$ where $s\geq0$ is an integer, $u$ is a unit in $k[[y]]$ (i.e. $u(0)\neq0$), and $w\in M[y]$ such that $\text{deg}_y\,w<s$, where $M=(x_1,\dots,x_n)\subset k[[x]]$ is the maximal ideal of $k[[x]]$. For any $g\in k[[x,y]]$ there are unique $h\in k[[x,y]]$ and $r\in k[[x]][y]$ such that $\text{deg}_y\,r<s$ and $g=hf+r$.
**Definition 2.** We say $f\in k[[x,y]]$ is regular of order $s$ at $y$ if $f$ satisfies the assumptions of the division theorem. (Notice that $f$ is regular of at y if and only if $f(0,\dots,0,y)\neq0$. Also, for any $f$, is $f$ is non-unit, then after a linear change of coordinates, $f$ can be regular in some variable.)
**Theorem 2. (Weierstrass Preparation Theorem)** Let $f\in k[[x,y]]$ be regular of order $s$ at $y$, then there is a unique Weierstrass polynomial $p$ of degree $s$ such that $f=vp$ for some unit in $k[[x,y]]$.
**Corollary.** $k[[x]]$ is a UFD. (Notice that for a general UFD $A$, $A[[x]]$ may not be a UFD.)
So we can try to prove that a power series is irreducible with the preparation and Eisenstein's criterion combined.
Also, the [[Newton Polygon]] is a powerful tool to investigate reducibility.
Now to calculate $k[[x_1,\dots,x_n]]/(f)$, we factor $f$ into irreducible power series $g_1^{p_1}\cdots g_r^{p_r}$ and consider the famous homomorphism$$
\begin{equation}
\theta:k[[x_1,\dots,x_n]]/(f)\rightarrow k[[x_1,\dots,x_n]]/(g_1^{p_1})\times\cdots\times k[[x_1,\dots,x_n]]/(g_r^{p_r})
\end{equation}
$$Since $k[[x_1,\dots,x_n]]$ is a UFD, this is always injective. This would be an isomorphism if $g_i^{p_i}$'s were mutually coprime.
Now we try to calculate rings of the form $A=k[[x_1,\dots,x_n]]/(g^p)$ where $g$ is irreducible. By above discussions, we could assume that $g$ is a Weierstrass polynomial in $x_n$. We can already say many things about this ring without further assumptions.
1. If $p=1$, then $A$ is an integral domain.
2. If $p\geq2$, then $A$ is not reduced and its reduced ring is $k[[x_1,\dots,x_n]]/(g)$.
3. $\text{dim}\,A=n-1$ since the height of $(g^p)$, by Krull's Principal Ideal Theorem, is $1$.
4. If $p\geq2$, then $A$ is non-regular. ($g^2$ is contained in $\mathfrak{m}=(x_1,\dots,x_n)$ and hence in this case, $\mathfrak{m}_A/\mathfrak{m}_A^2=\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m^2}$, which is of dimension $n$.)
Further discussions would certainly involve the specific form of $g$. And a powerful tool of this is the Newton's generalized binomial formula.
Cohen's structure theorem can be a powerful tool:
**Theorem. (Cohen Structure Theorem)** If $A$ is a complete regular local ring of dimension $n$ containing some field, then $A\cong k[[x_1,\dots,x_n]]$, where $k$ is the residue field of $A$.
See [[Section 10.160 (0323): The Cohen structure theorem—The Stacks project](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0323)] for a proof.

Now let's calculate the cases at the start. A simple calculation shows that all these four polynomials have one and only one singular point at $O=(0,0)$.
(a). Suppose $\sqrt{1-x^2}=s(x)\in k[[x]]$, then $f=(y^2-xs(x))(y^2+xs(x))$. Denote the two factors $g$ and $h$ respectively. By the proposition above, these are clearly irreducible. $(g,h)=(x,y^2)$, so $\theta$ is not surjective. Solving the quadratic equation $x^4-x^2+y=0$, we see that $x$ is expressible as a power series of $y$, so $k[[x,y]]/(g)\cong k[[t]]$, where the isomorphism is given by mapping $t$ to $y$. This result can also be prove by using Cohen's theorem. $k[[x,y]]/(g)$ is complete and local and by above discussion is of dimension 1, so we only need to show that it is regular. Since the relation $y^2=x\sqrt{1-x^2}=x-\frac{x^3}{2}+\cdots$ becomes $x=0$ modulo $(x,y)$, it is indeed regular. Similarly, $k[[x,y]]/(h)\cong k[[t]]$.
The algebraic fact that $f$ factor into two irreducible factors corresponds to the geometric interpretation that the curve has two analytic branches at $O$. Since the linear parts of $g$ and $h$ are $x$ and $-x$ respectively, we see that this is a tacnode (i.e. the two branches are tangent).
To further identify the image of $k[[x,y]]/(f)$ in $k[[t]]\times k[[t]]$, we need to calculate explicit formulas expressing $x$ as power series in $t=y$. To do so, we write $t^2=xs(x)$ as $x=t^2s(x)^{-1}=t^2(1+x^2/2+\cdots)$. And take $x\approx t^2$ as an approximation, then we get $x=t^2+t^6/2+h.o.t.$. This can be justified by the [[Lagrange Implicit Function Theorem.pdf]] for the zero characteristic case. Similarly, for $y^2+xs(x)=0$, we get $x=-t^2-t^6/2+h.o.t.$. Denote these two power series as $p$ and $q$ respectively. Composing the homomorphisms, we see that the image of $k[[x,y]]/(f)$ (denote $R$) is generated by the pairs $(t,t)$ and $(p(t),q(t))$. Denote $S=\{(u,v)\in k[[t]]\times k[[t]]\,|\,u(0)=v(0)\text{ and }u'(0)=v'(0)\}$, clearly this is a subring and it's easy to verify that $R\subset S$. We claim that $R=S$. Now any element in $S$ is of the form $(c_0+c_1t,c_0+c_1t)+(u_2(t),v_2(t))$, where $u_2$ and $v_2$ are dividable by $t^2$. So $S$ is generated over $k\times k$ by $(1,1),(t,t),(t^k,0)\text{ and }(0,t^k)$ for $k\geq2$. So it suffices to show that $(t^2,0)\in R$ and similarly $(0,t^2)\in R$. Since $(t,t)^2+(p(t),q(t))=(2t^2+h.o.t.,-t^6/2+h.o.t.)$ and we could eliminate the second component by powers of $(t,t)$ while the first component is always of the form $unit\cdot t^2$. Although this takes infinitely many steps, $R$ is complete, so these infinitely steps converge to $(t^2,0)\in R$ (or we could finish out the elimination by one single power series). This completes the proof.
Notice that our proof works as long as $x$ can be written as a power series in $y$ with initial term of order 2. After suitable linear change of coordinates, we could always reduce a tacnode to this case. Also the fact that our target ring $S$ contains only first order information implies that only first order information (i.e. constant and the tangent direction) matters. So for short, we assert that: suppose $f$ can be factored into two irreducible power series, then
1. If two components have same first order terms, then this is a tacnode.
2. Otherwise, this is a node.
This is a complete classification of analytically isomorphic classes for a two-branch intersecting singular point in a variety of dimension 1. Also, this can be generalized to multiple branches easily.
(b). We claim that their are power series such that $gh=xy-x^6-y^6$. Clearly we should take$$
\begin{align}
g&=x+g_5+\cdots\\
h&=y+h_5+\cdots
\end{align}
$$Then we need to solve $g_5y+h_5x=-x^6-y^6$, which has a solution since $-x^6-y^6$ is in $(x,y)$. Then we need to solve more equations which hold for same reasons. By the proposition above, $h$ and $g$ are irreducible. So we see that this is a node at $O$. And the completion of $\mathcal{O}_O$ is isomorphic to $S=\{(u,v)\in k[[t]]\times k[[t]]\,|\,u(0)=v(0)\}$.
(c). The principal part is $x^3-y^2$, which is irreducible as a polynomial, so by a theorem related to the [[Newton Polygon]], we see that $f$ is irreducible in $k[[x,y]]$. So this is a cusp. To calculate $\hat{\mathcal{O}}_O$, let $X=x\sqrt[3]{1-x}$ and $Y=y\sqrt{1+y^2}$. Since the second terms are units, we see that $k[[x,y]]/(f)\cong k[[X,Y]]/(X^2-Y^3)\cong k[[t]]$ via $X=t^3$ and $Y=t^2$.
(d). The principal part is $xy(x+y)$, so this is a triple point. Write $f=uvw$, with first order terms $x,y,x+y$ respectively. Similar to $(c)$, we can verify that $k[[x,y]]/(u)\cong k[[t]]$ since $u$ is a unit multiplying $x$ and so on. Similarly, we can prove that $k[[x,y]]/(f)$ is isomorphic to $S=\{(p,q,r)\in k[[t]]\times k[[t]]\times k[[t]]\,|\,p(0)=q(0)=r(0)\}$.
