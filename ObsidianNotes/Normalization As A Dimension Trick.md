This trick uses one simple fact: if $B$ is an integral extension of $A$, then $\text{dim}\,A=\text{dim}\,B$. So for a finitely generated $C$-algebras $B$, say generated by $x_1,\dots,x_n$, we could take $A=B[x_1^{d_1},\dots,x_n^{d_n}]$, $A=B[x_1, x_2-x_1^{d_2}, \dots, x_n-x_1^{d_n}]$  (often involves the ==separating degrees technique==, i.e. choose large $d_i$'s so that all monomials appear have different degree in $x_1$ after substitution), or other possible forms such that $B$ is integral over $A$ and some particular (prime) ideal contracts to a normalized (prime) ideal in $A$ or that $\text{dim}\,A$ is easier to calculate.

**Lemma 1.** Let $k$ be a field and $A=k[X_1,\dots,X_n]$, then the ideal $(X_1,\dots,X_i)$ is a prime ideal of height $i$, for $1\leq i\leq n$.
**Proof.** Consider the chain$$
(0)\subset(X_1)\subset(X_1,X_2)\subset\cdots\subset(X_1,\dots,X_i)\subset\cdots\subset(X_1,\dots,X_n)
$$$$\tag*{$\square$}$$**Theorem 2.** Let $B$ be a Noetherian ring, and let $A$ be a Noetherian subring over which $B$ is integral. Then
1) $\text{dim}\,A=\text{dim}\,B$,
2) for any $P\in \text{Spec}\,B$ we have $\text{ht}\,P\leq \text{ht}\,P\cap A$,
3) if, moreover, the going-down theorem holds between $A$ and $B$, for example, when $A$ and $B$ are integral domains and $A$ is integrally closed, then for any ideal $J$ of $B$ we have $\text{ht}\,J=\text{ht}\,J\cap A$.
**Proof.** See Matsumura, 13.C. and 5.E. $$\tag*{$\square$}$$**Theorem 3.** Let $A=k[X_1,\dots,X_n]$ be a polynomial ring over a field $k$, and let $I$ be an ideal of $A$ with $\text{ht}\,I=r$. Then we can choose $Y_1,...,Y_n\in A$ in such a way that
4) $A$ is integral over $k[Y]=k[Y_1,\dots,Y_n]$, and
5) $I\cap k[Y]=(Y_1,\dots,Y_r)$.
**Proof.** This proof is from Matsumura, 14.F. Induction on $r$. If $r=0$ then $I=(0)$ and we can take $Y_i=X_i$.
When $r=1$, let $Y_1=f(X)$ be any non-zero element of $I$. Write $f(X)=\sum_{i=1}^sa_iM_i(X)$, where $0\neq a_i\in k$ and $M_i(X)$ are distinct monomials in $X_1,\dots,X_n$ and take $n$ positive integers $d_1=1,d_2,\dots,d_n$. If $M(X)=\prod X_i^{a_i}$ then let us call the integer $\sum a_id_i$ the weight of the monomial $M(X)$. By a suitable choice of $d_2,\dots,d_n$ we can see to it that no two of the monomials $M_1,\dots,M_s$ that appear in $f(X)$ have the same weight. (If $p$ is a given prime number, we can take $d_2=p^{1/2},\dots,d_s=p^{\nu_s}$ where $\nu_i-\nu_{i-1}\quad(i=2,\dots,s;\nu_1=0)$ are large integers.) Put $Y_i=X_i-X_1^{d_i}\quad(i=2,\dots,n)$. Then$$
Y_1=f(X)=f(X_1,Y_2+X_1^{d_2},\dots,Y_n+X_1^{d_n})=a_iX_1^e+g(X_1,\dots,Y_2,\dots,Y_n)$$ where $g$ is a polynomial whose degree in $X_1$ is less than $e$ and $a_i$ is the coefficient of the term with highest weight in $f(X)$. Then $X_1$ is integral over $k[Y]$, and hence $X_i=Y_i+X_1^{d_i}\quad (i=2,\dots,n)$ are also integral over $k[Y]$. The ideal $(Y_1)$ of $k[Y]$ is prime of height 1, $(Y_1)\subset I\cap k[Y]$, and $\text{ht}\,I\cap k[Y]=\text{ht}\,I=1$ by **Theorem 1.** Therefore $(Y_1)=I\cap k[Y]$, as wanted.
When $r>1$, let $J$ be an ideal of $k[X]$ such that $J\subset I$, $\text{ht}\,J=r-1$. (The existence of such $J$ is easy to prove for any Noetherian ring and for any ideal $I$ of height $r$. Take $f_1\in I$ from outside of the minimal prime ideals, which exists since a Noetherian ring only has finitely many minimal primes and $J$ is not contained in the union of them by prime avoidance, and $f_2\in I$ from outside of the minimal prime over-ideals of $(f_1)$, which exists for similar reasons, and $f_3\in I$ from outside of the minimal prime over-ideals of $(f_1, f_2)$, and so on, and put $J=(f_1, \dots, f_{r-1})$. Then $\text{ht}\,J\leq r-1$, see Matsumura 12.I or p. 172 of notes on Algebra II. Also by the choice of $f_i$'s, $\text{ht}\,J\geq r-1$, so $\text{ht}\,J=r-1$.) By induction hypothesis there exists $Z_1,\dots,Z_n\in k[X]$ such that $k[X]$ is integral over $k[Z]$ and that $k[Z]\cap J=(Z_1,\dots,Z_{r-1})$. Put $I'=I\cap k[Z]$. Then $\text{ht}\,I'=\text{ht}\,I=r$, and so $I'\supsetneq(Z_1,\dots,Z_{r-1})$. Thus we can choose an element $0\neq f(Z_r,\dots,Z_n)$ of $I'$. Following the method we used for the case $r=1$, we put$$
Y_i=Z_i\quad(i<r),\quad Y_r=f(Z_r,\dots,Z_n),\quad Y_{r+j}=Z_{r+j}-Z_r^{e_j}\quad(1\leq j\leq n-r).
$$Then, for a suitable choice of $e_1,\dots,e_{n-r}$, $k[Z]$ is integral over $k[Y]$. Moreover, $I\cap k[Y]$ contains the prime ideal $(Y_1,\dots,Y_r)$ of height $r$ and so coincides with it. The proof is completed.$$\tag*{$\square$}$$**Remark.** The proof shows that we can choose the $Y_i$'s in such a way that $Y_{r+1},\dots Y_n$ have the form $Y_{r+j}=X_{r+j}+F_j(X_1,\dots,X_r)$, where $F_j$ is a polynomial with coefficients in the prime subring $k_0$ of $k$ (i.e. the canonical image of $\mathbb{Z}$ in $k$). If $\text{char}\,k=p>0$ then we can see to it that $F_j(X_1,\dots,X_r)\in k_0[X_1^p,\dots,X_r^p]$ for all $j$.

**Corollary 4.** (Noether's Normalization Lemma) See [[Noether's Normalization & Geometrical Interpretation]].
**Proof.** Write $A=k[X_1,\dots,X_n]/I$, and put $\text{ht}\,I=n-r$. According to the theorem there exist elements $Y_1,\dots,Y_n$ of $k[X_1,\dots,X_n]$ such that $k[X]$ is integral over $k[Y]$ and that $I\cap k[Y]=(Y_{r+1},\dots,Y_n)$. Putting $y_i=Y_i\,\,\text{mod }I\quad(1\leq i\leq r)$, we get the required result. Notice that we also have $r=\text{dim}\,A=\text{tr.deg}_k\,A$.$$\tag*{$\square$}$$**Definition.** A ring $A$ is said to be **catenary** if, for each pair of prime ideals $\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q}$ with $\mathfrak{p}\supset\mathfrak{q}$, $\text{ht}\,\mathfrak{p/q}$ is finite and is equal to the length of any maximal prime chain between $\mathfrak{p}$ and $\mathfrak{q}$. (When $A$ is Noetherian, the condition $\text{ht}\,\mathfrak{p/q}<\infty$ is automatically satisfied.) Thus if $A$ is a Noetherian domain the following conditions are equivalent:
1) $A$ is catenary,
2) for any pair of prime ideals $\mathfrak{p,q}$ such that $\mathfrak{p}\supset\mathfrak{q}$, we have $\text{ht}\,\mathfrak{p}=\text{ht}\,\mathfrak{q}+\text{ht}\,\mathfrak{p/q}$,
3) for any pair of prime ideals $\mathfrak{p,q}$ such that $\mathfrak{p}\supset\mathfrak{q}$ with $\text{ht}\,\mathfrak{p/q}=1$, we have $\text{ht}\,\mathfrak{p}=\text{ht}\,\mathfrak{q}+1$.
If $A$ is catenary, then any localization and holomorphic image of $A$ are also catenary.
A ring $A$ is said to be **universally catenary** if $A$ is Noetherian and if every $A$-algebra of finite type is catenary. Since any $A$-algebra of finite type is a homomorphic image of $A[X_1,\dots,X_n]$, a Noetherian ring $A$ is universally catenary if and only if $A[X_1,\dots,X_n]$ is catenary for every $n\geq0$.
If $A$ is u.c., so are the localizations and holomorphic images and any $A$-algebras of finite type.

**Corollary 5.** Let $A$ be a finitely generated algebra over a field $k$. Then
1) if $A$ is an integral domain, we have $\text{dim}\,A/\mathfrak{p}+\text{ht}\,\mathfrak{p}+\text{dim}\,A$ for any prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}$ of $A$, and
2) $A$ is universally catenary.
**Proof.** See Matsumura, 14.H., notice that 2) is a direct corollary of 1).$$\tag*{$\square$}$$Here're some applications of the Noether's Normalization:
**Corollary 6.** Let $k$ be a field and $A$ a finitely generated $k$-algebra, then $\text{dim}\,A=\text{tr.deg}_k\,A$.
Recall: 
1) If $A$ is a Noetherian ring, then $\text{dim}\,A[X_1,\dots,X_n]=\text{dim}\,A+n$. See Atiyah, Ch. 11, Exercise 7.
2) Let $f:B\rightarrow B'$ be a homomorphism of $A$-algebras, and let $C$ be an $A$-algebra. If $f$ is integral, prove that $f\otimes1:B\otimes_A C\rightarrow B'\otimes_A C$ is integral. See Atiyah, Ch. 5, Exercise 3.
**Corollary 7.** Let $k$ be a field, $A$ a finitely generated $k$-algebra and $C$ a Noetherian $k$-algebra, then $\text{dim}\,A\otimes_k C=\text{dim}\,A+\text{dim}\,C$.
**Proof.** Use Noether's Normalization and two propositions above.$$\tag*{$\square$}$$

**Corollary 8.** Let $Y_1,Y_2$ be two affine varieties of dimension $r,s$ in $\mathbb{A}_k^n$, where $k$ is a field. Then any irreducible component $W$ of $Y_1\cap Y_2$ has dimension $\leq r+s-n$. In particular, if $Y_2$ is a hypersurface, then $\text{dim}\,W=r-1$.
**Proof.** Let $W$ be an irreducible component of $Y_1\cap Y_2$, then $I(W)$ is a minimal prime ideal over $I(Y_1)+I(Y_2)$. Let $A=k[X_1,\dots\,X_n]$ and $Y_1,\dots,Y_n$ another set of indeterminates. Denote $\mathfrak{p}_1=I(Y_1),\mathfrak{p}_2=I(Y_2)$ and let $\mathfrak{p'}_2$ be the image of $\mathfrak{p}_2$ under the isomorphism mapping $X_i$ to $Y_i$. Let $I$ be the ideal of $k[X_1,\dots,X_n,Y_1,\dots,Y_n]$ generated by $\mathfrak{p}_1$ and $\mathfrak{p}_2$, and $D=(X_1-Y_1,\dots,X_n-Y_n)$.$$
k[X,Y]/I\cong A(Y_1)\otimes_k A(Y_2),\quad k[X,Y]/D\cong k[X].$$by **Corollary 6.** we have $\text{dim}\,k[X,Y]/I=r+s$. Writing $k[X,Y]=k[x,y]$ we have$$
k[X,Y]/(D+I)=k[x,y]/(x_1-y_1,\dots,x_n-y_n).$$Since $$
k[X,Y]/(D+I)=k[x,y]/(\mathfrak{p}_1+\mathfrak{p}_2),$$$\mathfrak{q}=I(W)$ corresponds to a minimal prime over-deal $Q$ of $I+D$ in $k[X,Y]$ such that $k[X]/\mathfrak{q}\cong k[X,Y]/Q$. Then $Q/I$ is a minimal prime over-ideal of $(x_1-y_1,\dots,x_n-y_n)$ of $k[x,y]$, hence $\text{ht}\,Q/I\leq n$. Therefore$$\text{dim}\,k[X]/\mathfrak{q}=\text{dim}\,k[x,y]/(Q/I)=\text{dim}\,k[x,y]-\text{ht}\,Q/I\geq r+s-n.$$$$\tag*{$\square$}$$
