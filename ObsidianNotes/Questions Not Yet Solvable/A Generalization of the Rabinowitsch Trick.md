#Algebraic-Geometry 
Hartshorne, I.4.4.2 is the geometric version of the trick. It state:
**Lemma.** Let $Y$ be a hypersurface in $\mathbb{A}^n$ given by the equation $f(x_1,\dots,x_n)=0$. Then $\mathbb{A}^n-Y$ is isomorphic to the hypersurface $H$ in $\mathbb{A}^{n+1}$ given by $x_{n+1}f=1$. In particular, $\mathbb{A}^n-Y$ is affine with affine ring $k[x_1,\dots,x_n]_f$.
#### Questions
1. Any possible generalizations? The broadest is when is a quasi-affine variety affine?
2. A more relevant one is what about $Y$ defined by a non-principal prime ideal?
3. Or what about common non-zeros (the intersection of complements of hyperplanes)?
4. Once we have established the affineness, what's the smallest possible embedding into a higher dimensional affine space in each conditions?
5. Projective version?
#### Solved Parts
Q. 3 is dumb, $D(f_1\cdots f_r)=D(f_1)\cap\cdots\cap D(f_r)$.
Proposition. (Projective version.) The non-zero set of a homogeneous polynomial is affine.
This is prove in Hartshorne's second chapter, $D_+(f)=\text{Spec}\,S_{(f)}$ Also here's another idea: consider a variation of the d-uple embedding$$
\begin{align}
\phi:D_+(f)&\longrightarrow\mathbb{A}^{N+1}\\
a&\longmapsto(\frac{M_0(a)}{f(a)},\dots,\frac{M_N(a)}{f(a)})
\end{align}$$which is an isomorphism onto a closed affine sub-scheme of $\mathbb{A}^{N+1}$. (Note that this is a very simplified description of a morphism between schemes, to have it making more sense one may consider it as the image of a morphism between varieties under the fully faithful functor from the category of varieties to the category of schemes.) This gives a boundary for the related Q. 4.
Here's something useful relevant to Q.2. Suppose $I=(f_1,\dots,f_r)$ (an ordinary ideal), denote $U=A^n-Z(I)$, consider$$
\begin{align}
\psi:U&\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{r-1}\\
x&\longmapsto[f_1(x),\dots,f_r(x)]
\end{align}$$
#### To Be Learned
**Theorem.** Let $X$ be an affine scheme, $Z$ a closed sub-scheme of $X$. Then $U=X-Z$ is affine if and only if $Z$ is a closed sub-scheme generated by a principal ideal.
The "if"-part is easy, $D(f)=\text{Spec}\,A_f$, and the proof uses the Rabinowitsch trick. For the only if part, see III.3.7 (Serre's Criterion).
