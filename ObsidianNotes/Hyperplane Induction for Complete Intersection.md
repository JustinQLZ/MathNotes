This is the solution for Hartshorne, Ex.III.9.6. This solution is a great practice for some recently learnt algebras.
**Proposition.** Let $Y\subset\mathbb{P}^n$ be a nonsingular variety of dimension $\geq2$ over an algebraically closed field $k$. Suppose $\mathbb{P}^{n-1}$ is a hyperplane in $\mathbb{P}^n$ which does not contain $Y$, and such that the scheme $Y'=Y\cap\mathbb{P}^{n-1}$ is also nonsingular. Then $Y$ is a complete intersection in $\mathbb{P}^n$ if and only if $Y'$ is a complete intersection in $\mathbb{P}^{n-1}$.

First we need a corollary of Hironaka's lemma:
**Lemma.** Let $A$ be a noetherian domain, which is a localization of an algebra of finite type over a field $k$. Let $t\in A$, and assume
1. $tA$ has only one minimal associated prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}$ (i.e. $\mathrm{minAss}_A\,A/tA=\{\mathfrak{p}\}$),
2. $t$ generates the maximal ideal of $A_{\mathfrak{p}}$,
3. $A/\mathfrak{p}$ is normal.
Then $\mathfrak{p}=tA$.
**Proof.** Let $\mathfrak{q}\subset A$ be any prime ideal.
*Case 1.* $t\notin\mathfrak{q}$. $\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{q}}=A_{\mathfrak{q}}=tA_{\mathfrak{q}}$.
*Case 2.* $t\in\mathfrak{q}$. Then by Hironaka's lemma (see Hartshorne, III.9.12) we have $\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{q}}=tA_{\mathfrak{q}}$.
So $\mathfrak{p}=tA$.$$\tag*{$\square$}$$**Remark.** Another excellent example of the ideal of global-local principal! This principal is really very general and useful.

Let $I\subset S=k[x_0,\dots,x_n]$ be the saturated ideal of $Y$ and $I'\subset S$ that of $Y'$, and assume that $\mathbb{P}^{n-1}$ is defined by $x_n$.
**Claim.** $I'=I+(x_n)$.
Clearly $I\subset I+(x_n)\subset I'$ and $I'$ is the saturation of $I+(x_n)$. Recall the following lemma for hyperplane sections:
**Lemma.** Let $X$ be a nonsingular projective variety of dimension $\geq2$ over an algebraically closed field $k$. Then any nonsingular hyperplane section of $X$ is connected, hence irreducible, and so is a nonsingular variety. (Hartshorne, III.7.9.1 and notes at p. 83.)
Therefore by notes at p. 79, $I'$ and $I$ are prime ideals. $S/I'$ is normal by Hartshorne, Ex.II.8.4.(b). Clearly $I'$ is the unique minimal associated prime ideal of $x_nS$.
**Claim.** $x_n$ generates $I'(S/I)_{I'}$. There exists $x_j\notin I'$. For any $\bar{a}/\bar{s}\in I'(S/I)_{I'}$, there exsits $m$ such that $x_j^ma=b+x_nc$ with $b\in I$. Then in $(S/I)_{I'}$ we have $\bar{a}/\bar{s}=\bar{x}_n(\bar{c}/x_j^ms)\in \bar{x}_n(S/I)_{I'}$. The other direction is trivial.
Then by the lemma above, $I'=I+(x_n)$.

**Claim.** Let $I''\subset S'=k[x_0,\dots,x_{n-1}]$ be the saturated ideal of $Y'$ in $\mathbb{P}^{n-1}$, then $I'=I''S+x_nS$.
By the first lemma of [[Projective Cone and Very Flat Family]], $I''[x_n]\subset S$ is saturated and defines the projective cone $C(Y')$. Clearly $S/(I''[x_n]+x_nS)=S'/I''$, so $I''[x_n]+(x_n)$ defines $Y'\subset\mathbb{P}^n$. Then by a similar argument, $I''[x_n]+x_nS=I'$.
Since $Y'$ is a complete intersection of codimension $r$ in $\mathbb{P}^{n-1}$, $I''=(f_1,\dots,f_r)$ with $f_i\in S'$. Therefore $I'=(f_1,\dots,f_r,x_n)=I+(x_n)$. We can write $f_i=g_i+x_nh_i$ with $g_i\in I$ and $h_i\in S$. Then $I'=(g_1,\dots,g_r,x_n)$.

**Claim.** $I=(g_1,\dots,g_r)=:J$.
Clearly $J\subset I$. Suppose $V_+(J)=Z_1\cup\cdots\cup Z_s$ with $Z_i$ irreducible. Then $Y'=V_+(J)\cap V_+(x_n)=\cup_i(Z_i\cap V_+(x_n))$. Since $Y'$ is irreducible, for any $i$ we have $Y'\subset Z_i$. For any closed point $P\in Y'$, $\mathrm{rank}\,J_P(g_1,\dots,g_r,x_n)=r+1$ since $Y'$ is nonsingular. Therefore $\mathrm{rank}\,J_P(g_1,\dots,g_r)=r$.
**Claim.** $\mathrm{ht}\,J=r$.
Since its generated by $r$ elements, we have $\mathrm{ht}\,J\leq r$. Suppose $\mathrm{ht}\,J<r$, then since $I'/J$ is generated by one element, $\mathrm{ht}\,I'<r+1$. But $I'$ defines $Y'$, which has codimension $r+1$ in $\mathbb{P}^{n+1}$. Thus $\mathrm{ht}\,J\geq r$.
Therefore $\mathrm{ht}\,J=r=\mathrm{rank}\,J_P(g_1,\dots,g_r)$ and $V_+(J)$ is nonsingular at $P$. ==In particular, V_+(J) has only one irreducible component at P.== Combining $Y'\subset Z_i$ for any $i$, we see that $s=1$, i.e. $V_+(J)$ is irreducible. The unique minimal prime over $J$ is $I$ since $\mathrm{ht}\,I=r$.
On the other hand, by unmixedness, $J$ has no embedded primes, i.e. $S/J$ satisfies $S_1$. Also, since $S/J$ is regular at $P$, it is so at the generic point, i.e. $S/J$ satisfies $R_0$. Therefore, $k[x_0,\dots,x_n]/J$ is reduced, and thus a domain. Therefore $J$ is a prime, equal to $I$. By definition, $Y$ is a complete intersection in $\mathbb{P}^n$.

**Remark.** Arguments on regularity and height of $J$ exhibits the power of commutative algebra! Try to learn this style of proof!
