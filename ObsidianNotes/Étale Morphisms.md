**Definition.** Let $f:X\rightarrow Y$be a morphism of schemes of finite type over $k$. It is *étale* if it is smooth of relative dimension 0. It is *unramified* at $x\in X$ if, letting $y=f(x)$, we have $\mathfrak{m}_y\cdot\mathcal{O}_x=\mathfrak{m}_x$, and $k(x)$ is a separable algebraic extension of $k(y)$. It is unramified if it is unramified at every point of $X$.

**Lemma.** *(Matsumura, 27.B)* Let $K/k$ be a finitely generated extension of fields, then $\Omega_{K/k}=0$ iff $K$ is separably algebraic over $k$.
**Lemma.** Let $f:X\rightarrow Y$ be a morphism and $x\in X$ with $y=f(x)$, then there is a surjective $k(x)$-linear map$$
\Omega_{X/Y}\otimes k(x)\rightarrow \Omega_{k(x)/k(y)}
$$In particular, if $\Omega_{X/Y}\otimes k(x)=0$, then $\Omega_{k(x)/k(y)}=0$.
**Proof.** Let $Z$ be the irreducible closed subscheme defined by $x$. By the second exact sequence, there is a surjective map $\Omega_{X/Y}\otimes \mathcal{O}_Z\rightarrow\Omega_{Z/Y}$. By the first exact sequence, there is an isomorphism $\Omega_{Z/Y}\otimes k(x)\rightarrow\Omega_{k(x)/Y}$. Again by the first exact sequence, there is a surjective map $\Omega_{k(x)/Y}\rightarrow\Omega_{k(x)/k(y)}$. Then the composition of these maps give the desired map.$$\tag*{$\square$}$$**Remark.** This lemma will not be used. It's only here because I was curious about it.

**Corollary.** Let $f:X\rightarrow Y$ be a morphism of  locally finite type, then $f$ is unramified at $x$ iff $\Omega_{X/Y}\otimes k(x)=0$. In particular, $f$ is unramified iff $\Omega_{X/Y}=0$.
**Proof.** By localizing and applying Nakayama's lemma, we need to show that if $A$ and $B$ are local rings with maximal ideals $\mathfrak{m}_A$ and $\mathfrak{m}_B$ respectively, $f:A\rightarrow B$ is a local homomorphism such that $f^{-1}(\mathfrak{m}_B)=\mathfrak{m}_A$, and $B$ is finitely generated as an $A$-algebra, then $f$ is unramified if and only if $\Omega_{B/A}=0$.
Suppose $f$ is unramified, i.e. $\mathfrak{m}_AB=\mathfrak{m}_B$ and $k_B/k_A$ is a separable algebraic extension. Then $\Omega_{k_B/k_A}=0$. For any $b\in\mathfrak{m}_B$, to show that $\mathrm{d}b\otimes 1=0\in\Omega_{B/A}\otimes_B k_B$, we can assume that $b=ab'$ for $a\in A$ and $b'\in B$. Then $\mathrm{d}b\otimes1=a\mathrm{d}b'\otimes 1=\mathrm{d}b'\otimes a=0$. So by the second exact sequence and Nakayama's lemma, $\Omega_{B/A}=0$.
Suppose $\Omega_{B/A}=0$, then by the second exact sequence $\Omega_{k_B/k_A}=0$ and hence $k_B/k_A$ is separably algebraic by the first lemma. Let $\mathfrak{m}_AB=I\subset\mathfrak{m}_A$.
