## 1. Basic Configurations

The configuration space is a smooth orientable Riemann manifold $(M,g)$. Let $FM$ be the frame bundle, or the $SO(n)$-principal bundle of $TM$, where $n=\mathrm{dim}\,M$.

A key observation is that $SO(n)$ is not simply connected and it has a $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$ universal covering Lie group $\pi:Spin(n)\rightarrow SO(n)$. A **spin structure** on $M$ is a double covering $\pi:P\rightarrow FM$, where $P$ is a $Spin(n)$-principal bundle and $\pi(p\cdot h)=\pi(p)\cdot\pi(h),\forall p\in P,h\in Spin(n)$. The obstruction of a spin structure is $w_2(M)\in H^2(M;\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})$ and the set of all spin structures has a natural $H^1(M;\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})$-torsor structure given by $P^{\epsilon}=P\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}}L_{\epsilon}$ for any $\epsilon\in H^1(M;\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})$.

Complex irreducible representations of $Spin(n)$ are classified by half 

Fix a spin structure on $M$, then let $\mathcal{H}=\Gamma_{L^2}(M,E_s)$. And we can consider quantum physics over this Hilbert space.

Typically in non-relative-theory quantum physics, $M=\mathbb{R}^3$ and $g$ is the canonical metric.
